# Topic: OT and IoT security

## Meta-Summary

The records portray OT and IoT security as a broad cyber-physical discipline spanning industrial automation, embedded firmware, consumer appliances, transportation, buildings, healthcare, energy, water, maritime systems, aviation, and spacecraft. Across these settings, the same structural weaknesses recur: implicit trust in networks and protocols, unauthenticated control messages, weak or shared credentials, insecure update paths, opaque firmware, exposed debug interfaces, unsupported software, white-label supply chains, and cloud or mobile backends that can become more consequential than the devices themselves.

A central finding is that security boundaries routinely fail at interfaces. IT/OT gateways, cellular modules, dealer platforms, cloud dashboards, engineering workstations, zero-touch provisioning systems, out-of-band controllers, and protocol translators all create paths from ordinary networks into physical control. Examples range from OPC UA authentication bypass and deterministic TSN signal manipulation to compromised cameras, EV chargers, marine autopilots, vehicle ECUs, medical devices, and satellite software [record_id:7] [record_id:2593] [record_id:2990]. The consequences extend beyond confidentiality: records demonstrate or allege tracking, surveillance, persistent denial of service, safety-control bypass, navigation manipulation, unauthorized vehicle operation, grid disruption, patient impact, and destruction of hardware [record_id:32] [record_id:43] [record_id:2083] [record_id:2609].

The research also shows a methodological transition. Hardware teardown, firmware extraction, Ghidra-based reverse engineering, SDR analysis, fault injection, protocol fuzzing, emulation, and testbed validation remain foundational. Alongside them, researchers increasingly use scalable rehosting, internet-wide measurement, machine learning, multi-agent defense, and bounded LLM assistance. The strongest AI-related lesson is not that models replace expertise, but that they can accelerate well-scoped ranking, reverse engineering, and triage when researchers provide reliable tooling, constraints, and human validation [record_id:2206] [record_id:2619] [record_id:2629].

Defensively, the records favor asset visibility, segmentation that reflects operational constraints, behavioral baselining, safe coverage validation, cyber-informed engineering, specialized OT incident response, and continuity planning. They repeatedly caution against treating every physical failure as cyber-induced, relying on security through obscurity, or assuming that compliance, encryption, isolation, and safety certification automatically provide security [record_id:2072] [record_id:2995] [record_id:3002].

## Research Landscape

Most records are conference abstracts from Black Hat, DEF CON villages and main tracks, BSides Las Vegas, CAMLIS, and smaller AI-focused events. The collection is heavily weighted toward 2025–2026 presentations. It therefore captures proposed talks, demonstrations, tools, and preliminary findings more often than peer-reviewed papers or independently reproduced results. Many records promise live demonstrations, proof-of-concept releases, CVEs, datasets, or open-source tools, but the abstracts do not themselves establish that every claim was reproduced.

Several source patterns dominate:

1. **Device- and protocol-specific vulnerability research.** Researchers tear down a product or protocol, recover firmware, reverse proprietary communications, identify vulnerabilities, and build an exploit chain. Representative targets include OPC UA, TETRA, EV chargers, cameras, routers, locks, radios, tractors, maritime controllers, medical devices, and spacecraft [record_id:60] [record_id:68] [record_id:1931] [record_id:1983] [record_id:2103].

2. **Full-ecosystem assessments.** These studies connect devices to mobile applications, cloud APIs, provisioning infrastructure, OEM relationships, and operational workflows. The Xiaomi scooter work spans BMS firmware, BLE, mobile software, and ransomware infrastructure; the GPS-tracker research connects dozens of brands to shared backends; and solar, camera, and transport studies show how ostensibly distinct products inherit common backend or OEM weaknesses [record_id:43] [record_id:1978] [record_id:2646].

3. **Operational and sector-level security.** Water, energy, healthcare, food, emergency services, maritime operations, and public transportation appear as interdependent lifeline systems rather than isolated technical targets. These records emphasize cascading failures, continuity of operations, regulation, workforce limitations, and cross-sector incident command [record_id:2413] [record_id:2434] [record_id:2451] [record_id:2534].

4. **Defensive experimentation and simulation.** CAMLIS work explores autonomous cyber-defense in vehicle platooning and maritime control environments, while later records introduce ship, space, nuclear, and industrial testbeds for detection and response research [record_id:136] [record_id:163] [record_id:2108] [record_id:3024].

5. **Training and community activity.** Villages, CTFs, badge talks, introductory reverse-engineering sessions, and hands-on workshops lower the barrier to embedded and ICS research. These are weaker as evidence of vulnerability prevalence but important evidence of an expanding practitioner community [record_id:1871] [record_id:2147] [record_id:2171] [record_id:3132].

The topic boundary is intentionally broad. Some secondary records concern devices adjacent to conventional OT, such as NAS appliances, BMCs, USB peripherals, hardware wallets, access-control systems, e-readers, bioimplants, and abandoned game consoles. They remain relevant because they illustrate embedded trust, firmware longevity, supply-chain reuse, physical interfaces, and network-edge compromise [record_id:1850] [record_id:2670] [record_id:3020].

## Major Themes And Trends

### Cyber-physical impact replaces data loss as the central risk measure

The records consistently distinguish OT and IoT security from ordinary application security by emphasizing effects on safety, availability, mission assurance, and physical processes. EV charger testing intentionally examines whether compromised equipment can bypass safety mechanisms and cause destruction or latent hazards [record_id:32]. Scooter ransomware changes BMS thresholds and reportedly reduces battery capacity while creating a fire and safety risk [record_id:43]. Tractor brake-controller exploitation is described as affecting speed indication, steering, and automatic shifting in a moving truck [record_id:2609]. Automotive Ethernet flooding disrupts wipers, headlights, infotainment, and vehicle movement [record_id:3054].

Comparable risks appear in marine navigation, aviation, medicine, and robotics. Unsigned marine-autopilot firmware permits persistent code execution and CAN injection [record_id:2083]. CPDLC message injection exploits unauthenticated aviation instructions [record_id:2861], while voice cloning targets trust in air-traffic communications [record_id:2047]. Pacemaker, insulin-pump, fertility-analyzer, DICOM, and medical-device kill-chain records frame compromise in terms of patient harm, alert suppression, diagnosis integrity, and continuity of care rather than CVSS alone [record_id:2649] [record_id:2965] [record_id:3011] [record_id:3019] [record_id:3067].

Several records warn against sensationalism. Grid disturbances are often rooted in control dynamics, equipment failure, or poor recovery planning rather than hostile code [record_id:2072]. Evidence-based ICS malware analysis similarly stresses that true process-targeting malware is rare and that public repositories contain benign research tools, CTF artifacts, and irrelevant samples [record_id:2053]. Together, these talks argue for physics-aware attribution: cyber claims should be tested against system behavior, operational context, and physical safeguards.

### Legacy trust assumptions survive in modern systems

Many protocols were designed for trusted or isolated environments and remain weak after being connected to IP networks, cloud services, or commodity radios. Modbus, CIP, DNP3, NMEA 2000, DICOM, IPMI, CPDLC, AIS, and proprietary industrial protocols commonly lack sender authentication or assume trusted network placement [record_id:2116] [record_id:2973] [record_id:3019] [record_id:3057].

Even newer technologies reproduce this pattern. CC-Link IE TSN prioritizes timing and availability, allowing an adversary who predicts synchronization values to inject accepted cyclic traffic [record_id:2593]. ISO 15118 improves parts of EV charging security but also shifts and creates attack surfaces [record_id:42]. Thread encrypts traffic, yet deterministic Matter packet sizes allegedly reveal building topology and device behavior [record_id:2638]. OPC UA supplies native cryptographic security, but protocol-level flaws undermine authentication across implementations [record_id:7] [record_id:2140].

The same problem appears in physical security. TTLock’s offline time model enables revocation bypass and credential resurrection [record_id:2899]. Saflok systems rely on obscure offline authentication and expose programming structures [record_id:1983] [record_id:3056]. Hotel, elevator, safe, alarm, RFID, and building access records show that physical security frequently rests on predictable credentials, exposed service interfaces, weak revocation, or insufficiently protected controllers [record_id:1999] [record_id:2002] [record_id:2697] [record_id:3007].

### IT/OT convergence creates hidden and bidirectional paths

The records repeatedly reject a simple model in which OT sits safely behind a firewall. Protocol gateways can expose devices on the far side through CIP forwarding or DNP3 address discovery [record_id:2104]. OT DMZs fail through jump hosts, remote-access tools, Citrix, RDP relays, poor segmentation, and credential reuse [record_id:2112]. Multi-interface hosts, BMCs, KVM systems, serial servers, printers, thermostats, and buses create accidental bridges that can be more important than documented firewall rules [record_id:2828].

Cellular connectivity is especially important. Researchers describe taking control of cellular modules, intercepting MCU-to-modem traffic, and abusing the device’s trusted backend relationship to attack cloud and private infrastructure [record_id:44] [record_id:3058]. Surprise cellular modems appeared in ship-to-shore crane assessments [record_id:2065], while a Polish energy investigation reportedly followed forgotten remote-access hardware and private APN connectivity into nominally isolated infrastructure [record_id:2895]. Maritime non-terrestrial cellular networks may carry terrestrial 4G/5G weaknesses out to sea [record_id:3029].

Cloud and enterprise applications also act as control paths. Automotive dealer-platform API flaws reportedly enabled ownership reassignment and remote vehicle control [record_id:2036] [record_id:2099]. Maritime cloud dashboards can provide remote writes to propulsion or ballast systems [record_id:2994]. Solar APIs, transport administration panels, GPS-tracker backends, and camera clouds similarly expand physical attack surfaces far beyond local device firmware [record_id:1978] [record_id:2714] [record_id:2903] [record_id:2949].

### Firmware and embedded supply chains amplify one flaw across fleets

Supply-chain reuse is one of the strongest cross-record themes. Chipset SDK bugs persist through many vendors and product generations because downstream manufacturers rarely update inherited code [record_id:2073]. A USB SDK flaw in a secure hardware wallet may affect unrelated payment terminals and card readers built from the same reference code [record_id:2603]. Outdated IoT SDKs are also blamed for dozens of modem vulnerabilities [record_id:1906].

White-label manufacturing obscures common ownership and infrastructure. Webcams trace back through distributors and OEMs to shared Linux firmware and broken update mechanisms [record_id:1925]. GPS products marketed under numerous brands share Shenzhen-based backends and architectural flaws [record_id:2646] [record_id:2903]. A camera ODM reportedly supplies hundreds of brands while retaining architectural access to device streams and cloud data [record_id:2949]. Solar products are rebadged across vendors while sharing cloud services, hardcoded credentials, unsigned updates, and Modbus weaknesses [record_id:1978].

The records also broaden “supply chain” beyond vendor update servers. HMI project files, templates, DLLs, fonts, backups, and communication policies can compromise engineering workstations before projects are deployed [record_id:2990]. Returned IoT products may retain modified firmware if retailers fail to sanitize them [record_id:2131] [record_id:2132]. Hardware bills of materials are proposed to expose chip provenance and hidden semiconductor dependencies [record_id:2034]. UAiRT illustrates the corresponding hardware threat: a cheap implant soldered to UART and power lines can establish an out-of-band root channel that network monitoring cannot see [record_id:3045].

### Update, lifecycle, and disclosure failures create “forever-day” risk

Embedded devices often remain deployed longer than vendor support, and many cannot be patched without operational risk. AirPlay-enabled IoT devices may remain vulnerable for years or never receive updates [record_id:80]. Legacy TelnetD and Samba flaws may persist in forgotten routers and printers [record_id:2585] [record_id:2902]. PackBot software reportedly retained old Linux and Python 2.5 components across generations [record_id:2596] [record_id:2886]. Satellite software faces constrained maintenance windows, validation costs, and mission risk after launch [record_id:2984] [record_id:3022].

End-of-life records frame unsupported firmware as a policy failure rather than merely an asset-management problem. “Forever-day” vulnerabilities persist in healthcare, manufacturing, energy, and transportation [record_id:2435]. Proposed “graceful exit” policies would require support-period disclosure and replacement or transition plans when vendors abandon products [record_id:2764]. Industrial disclosure also has asymmetric timelines: a researcher can publish a CVE long before operators can safely patch dozens of device models [record_id:3074].

The tractor recall research raises a related transparency issue. Binary differences allegedly showed that a “noise” recall also removed exploitable parsing and interrupt-handling code, effectively making it a security patch without associated CVEs [record_id:2609] [record_id:2943]. This suggests an open question about when safety remediation must also be disclosed as cybersecurity remediation.

### Mobility and transportation dominate the target set

Automotive work includes ECUs, head units, CAN anomaly detection, TPMS tracking, odometer manipulation, emissions testing, dealer systems, keyless entry, aftermarket alarms, and charging infrastructure [record_id:83] [record_id:2037] [record_id:2048] [record_id:2051] [record_id:2091] [record_id:2102]. Newer records move from isolated vehicle components to ecosystem-wide trust: BLE alarms share a global key across roughly 1.4 million cars [record_id:2947], rolling-code rollback enables fob cloning [record_id:3066], and V2G logic can allegedly be manipulated to discharge a locked EV [record_id:2933].

Public transportation records similarly move from fare media to whole operational ecosystems. Smart-bus attacks begin with passenger Wi-Fi and reach routers, ADAS/APTS communications, displays, personal data, and backend APIs [record_id:2121]. A city-scale study reports access to buses, taxis, DVRs, cards, real-time tracking, and administration systems [record_id:2714]. Transit RFID research demonstrates that obscured card formats and checksums do not provide durable security [record_id:2883].

Maritime security is unusually prominent. Records cover shipboard red teams, NMEA 2000 fingerprinting and intrusion detection, VSAT firmware, marine engines, autopilots, communications resilience, OSINT, incident response, cloud dashboards, dark-fleet operations, and safety governance [record_id:1946] [record_id:2033] [record_id:2039] [record_id:2049] [record_id:2057] [record_id:2133]. The recurring concern is that navigation, propulsion, communications, and enterprise systems are interconnected but poorly inventoried.

Aviation and space form another growing cluster. Space systems are increasingly treated as critical infrastructure with attack surfaces spanning ground stations, mission software, radios, onboard buses, and supply chains [record_id:16] [record_id:2046] [record_id:2103]. Aviation records cover on-wing Wi-Fi investigations, AM radio impersonation, CPDLC injection, AFDX, electronic flight systems, Remote ID, and accident-data recovery [record_id:1892] [record_id:2861] [record_id:3005] [record_id:3086].

### Surveillance, privacy, and device ownership are security issues

Cameras and tracking devices recur because compromise yields both an internal-network foothold and intimate physical intelligence. Axis.Remoting flaws permit pre-authentication RCE and passive organizational mapping [record_id:68] [record_id:2088] [record_id:2146]. Dahua and Hikvision records address camera RCE or unauthorized RTSP access [record_id:2050] [record_id:3023]. Flock Safety research alleges dozens of vulnerabilities and publicly exposed camera feeds [record_id:2920].

Consumer and institutional devices gather more data than users expect. School vape detectors, smart-home devices, robot vacuums, tracking beacons, and fertility analyzers expose location, audio, images, room layouts, or health information [record_id:1984] [record_id:3034] [record_id:3065] [record_id:3067]. Thread traffic analysis suggests encryption can protect content while leaking device identity, topology, and activity through metadata [record_id:2638]. The collection therefore treats privacy leakage as a precursor to physical targeting, stalking, surveillance, and operational reconnaissance—not merely a compliance problem.

### Threat actors are converging, but evidence varies

Several strategic records argue that the OT threat landscape has shifted from predominantly state-focused concern to overlap among states, ransomware operators, hacktivists, and low-skill actors using commoditized capabilities [record_id:2020] [record_id:2029]. Critical-infrastructure discussions cite Volt Typhoon, Cyber Avengers, Sandworm, and APT44 as examples of pre-positioning, living-off-the-land, and disruptive intent [record_id:2413] [record_id:2427] [record_id:2095] [record_id:2113].

More concrete incident narratives include an alleged attempt to incapacitate nuclear control systems [record_id:2544], physical red-team access to grid-control servers [record_id:2546], and destructive activity against Polish renewable and combined heat-and-power sites [record_id:2895] [record_id:3009]. However, many strategic abstracts provide few technical indicators, and some use dramatic language without enough raw evidence to distinguish verified incident details from scenario framing [record_id:2070]. Downstream researchers should separate demonstrated exploit chains from keynote-level threat claims.

## Methods, Tools, And Approaches Discussed

### Hardware teardown and firmware acquisition

The foundational workflow is still physical inspection, interface discovery, firmware extraction, and layered reverse engineering. Researchers use JTAG, UART, SWD, SPI, chip-off extraction, logic analyzers, debug ports, voltage glitching, and fault injection [record_id:2008] [record_id:2081] [record_id:3132]. Bare-metal analysis reconstructs board-support packages through SVD files, HAL functions through type recovery and pattern matching, and RTOS behavior through Ghidra BSim [record_id:2081].

Firmware encryption is treated as a speed bump rather than a reliable boundary. One workflow examines common encryption mistakes directly [record_id:2078]; another reconstructs vendor decryption by locating and emulating the vendor’s own cryptographic functions rather than reimplementing them [record_id:3077]. Camera, surveillance, medical, and appliance studies combine teardown with filesystem release, firmware repacking, and custom update generation [record_id:1984] [record_id:3011].

Hardware attack methods include lockstep glitching against automotive processors [record_id:83], voltage faults against post-quantum signature verification [record_id:65], laser fault injection and logic-state imaging [record_id:2479], TrustZone-M single-stepping side channels [record_id:2601], and timing attacks against OMAP-L138 trusted execution code [record_id:2856]. These records demonstrate that secure boot, lockstep execution, TEEs, and modern cryptography remain vulnerable when implementations expose physical or microarchitectural side channels.

### Emulation, rehosting, fuzzing, and differential analysis

Rehosting is becoming a central scaling technique. LightTouch attempts high-fidelity embedded Linux execution by patching kernels and QEMU machines while leaving userspace firmware intact [record_id:1981]. Bare-metal rehosting enabled fuzzing of Tesla Wall Connector firmware and discovery of update-path RCE and secure-boot bypass [record_id:2656]. U-Boot research compares hardware-in-the-loop, emulation, and native binary fuzzing [record_id:3104].

State-aware fuzzing addresses modern protocol complexity. Dependency-aware baseband fuzzing combines emulation with iterative symbolic analysis to discover state variables and preconditions, reportedly yielding seven new vulnerabilities [record_id:73]. The autonomous-vehicle scenario fuzzer iteratively creates malicious teleoperation trajectories, conflicting guidance, and environmental perturbations while measuring whether safety logic rejects them [record_id:55]. Binary differential analysis of tractor recall firmware uses IDA Pro, Python automation, and QBinDiff to identify removed vulnerable code [record_id:2609] [record_id:2943].

### Radio and protocol analysis

SDR-based methods recur across LoRa, TETRA, goTenna, weather alerts, TPMS, aviation, satellites, and long-range Wi-Fi. Meshtastic research traces frames from chirps through GNU Radio, Wireshark, Protobuf, and AES payloads, then crafts valid packets in Python [record_id:1884]. Related records use Meshtastic for low-bandwidth command and control, inspect a conference mesh, and provide village training [record_id:1895] [record_id:2944] [record_id:2149].

Commodity SDR hardware supports key-store poisoning in goTenna [record_id:1952], public-alert spoofing [record_id:1894], TPMS tracking and ECU spoofing [record_id:2048], CPDLC ground-station impersonation [record_id:2861], and satellite protocol exploitation [record_id:2877]. Ubiquiti AirMax work reverses proprietary 802.11 extensions and converts devices into reconnaissance tools [record_id:2624] [record_id:2901]. The broader lesson is that “adjacent” RF attacks may operate from kilometers away and should not be assessed like near-field physical attacks.

Industrial-protocol research ranges from introductory CIP exploitation [record_id:2116] [record_id:2973] to OPC UA cryptographic attacks [record_id:2140], TSN timing manipulation [record_id:2593], NMEA 2000 fingerprinting [record_id:2049], and cross-gateway asset discovery [record_id:2104]. Industrial protocol tools are increasingly packaged into safe simulation or emulation frameworks rather than directed at live infrastructure.

### Internet-wide measurement and asset discovery

Several records use large-scale scanning to move beyond anecdotes. Tunneling research scanned IPv4 and part of IPv6, identifying approximately 4.3 million hosts usable as one-way proxies and an ONT crash condition [record_id:9]. BMC research scanned 15,000 internet-facing and 125,000 internal devices, finding widespread RAKP hash disclosure and default or weak passwords; it releases OOBscan [record_id:2670] [record_id:2918].

IRONMAP reports more than 5.5 million OT-facing assets and deep protocol enumeration across CIP, Modbus, S7, DNP3, IEC-104, OPC UA, BACnet, FINS, and other protocols [record_id:3062]. A data-center study correlates geolocation and banners to identify 6,300 exposed industrial systems near 1,063 facilities [record_id:3010]. These studies provide stronger prevalence evidence than single-device demonstrations, although their classification accuracy, legal constraints, and active-query safety require scrutiny.

### Detection, baselining, and incident response

Detection methods increasingly use time, topology, process context, and device behavior. A sticky HDP-HMM models heterogeneous cyber-physical logs without requiring detailed architecture knowledge [record_id:186]. Lightweight correlation builds causal sequences across IoT sensors, devices, and cloud services without LLMs [record_id:1935]. Automotive CAN work models inter-signal correlation directly from raw payloads [record_id:2102].

Maritime NMEA 2000 research constructs deterministic device fingerprints [record_id:2049] and later an LSTM autoencoder ensemble for timing, payload, frequency, and device behavior [record_id:3057]. Space defenses derive Indicators of Behavior from SPARTA techniques [record_id:2105] and implement them in the SpaceCOP onboard IDS [record_id:3111]. OT hunting guidance favors hypothesis-driven investigations that account for safety, segmentation, real-time constraints, and coordination with operators [record_id:2980].

Operational programs include specialized OT SOCs [record_id:2079], MOSAICS monitoring and safe asset discovery for naval systems [record_id:2075], deception aligned to MITRE Engage [record_id:2095], and maritime NOC/SOC integration for asset intelligence and baseline development [record_id:3031]. ICSForge generates safe industrial traffic and PCAPs for 500-plus scenarios across ten protocols, mapping them to MITRE ATT&CK for ICS so defenders can validate sensors, firewalls, ACLs, and detections without touching production devices [record_id:2995].

### Simulation, exercises, and autonomous defense

Co-Decyber decomposes a large OT action space among cooperating agents and demonstrates recovery from GPS spoofing in a vehicle platoon [record_id:136]. IPMSRL applies multi-agent reinforcement learning to a maritime integrated platform management simulation, with MAPPO outperforming IPPO under the reported conditions [record_id:163]. NICSSIM extends this pattern to simulated small modular reactor fleets with separate analysis and remediation agents, deterministic guardrails, an independent safety auditor, and human authorization [record_id:3024].

Moonlight Defender combines satellites, ranges, virtual spacecraft, enterprise systems, and ICS/OT components for purple-team exercises [record_id:2071]. A virtual-ship architecture combines full-mission simulation, subsystem emulation, traffic generation, exploit access points, and analytics [record_id:2108]. Healthcare tabletop and CTF records emphasize that operations continue after compromise and require clinicians, executives, and defenders to make constrained choices [record_id:3001] [record_id:3131].

### AI assistance: useful when bounded, dangerous when unconstrained

The records show both productivity and failure. General-purpose coding assistants hallucinated board-support packages, copied wrong files, and failed to reason about hardware until the researcher manually created a valid STM32 project and limited the model to small insertions [record_id:2206]. This supports a repeated recommendation: constrain AI to inspectable subtasks with strong tooling and deterministic validation.

SiftRank repeatedly ranks decompiled functions for a specified vulnerability class, reportedly improving precision and finding a diagnostic RCE endpoint in commercial firmware [record_id:2619]. Other work uses “waypoints” required by Bluetooth specifications to automate firmware reverse engineering [record_id:2930], or MCP servers and playbooks to give agents access to Wi-Fi, BLE, firmware, and hardware tools [record_id:2986]. Wairz similarly exposes firmware and bench workflows to agents while distinguishing useful automation from work still better done manually [record_id:3026].

The strongest claims come from semi-autonomous IoT research that reports 30 vulnerabilities across two targets and a fleet-wide kill chain, but also an unconstrained agent that modified and disabled a live device [record_id:2629]. Plan R and related beginner-oriented talks suggest AI is lowering the barrier to IoT assessment [record_id:2986] [record_id:3026], while AI-generated industrial protocol actions raise concern that protocol-native process manipulation may become easier to compose [record_id:3046].

Defensive AI includes local, quantized models for air-gapped IoT log triage [record_id:2788], local OT models where data cannot leave the site [record_id:3028], and bounded aerospace agents for log analysis [record_id:2978]. Physical-AI and medical telemetry records warn that sensor spoofing or prompt injection can translate model manipulation into unsafe actions [record_id:2348] [record_id:2971].

## Notable Talks, Records, And Evidence

The OPC UA work is important because it targets a protocol explicitly relied upon for cryptographic protection between OT trust zones. Two protocol flaws are described as practical authentication bypasses across multiple implementations, involving signing oracles, padding oracles, and timing amplification [record_id:7] [record_id:2140]. This is stronger than a single implementation bug, though the abstracts do not enumerate affected products or remediation status.

The TETRA research is similarly significant. It combines reverse engineering of proprietary end-to-end encryption, seven vulnerabilities, key extraction, covert implants, voice injection, replay, weakened keys, and packet injection into SCADA networks [record_id:60]. Its importance comes from TETRA’s deployment across police, military, ports, airports, pipelines, transport, electricity, and water.

EV charging forms one of the best-supported thematic clusters. Product destruction testing examines safety-control bypass across seven chargers [record_id:32]; ISO 15118 analysis addresses systemic standard-level risk [record_id:42]; QCA7000 modem research demonstrates persistent denial of service, communication hijacking, and code execution [record_id:1931] [record_id:2145]; Tesla bare-metal rehosting connects charger RCE and secure-boot bypass to a proposed cross-device worm [record_id:2656]; and V2G research targets BMS logic for unauthorized discharge [record_id:2933]. Collectively, these records show risk at hardware, protocol, firmware, cloud, vehicle, and grid levels.

The camera and tracking records provide unusually clear examples of supply-chain concentration. Axis research offers a protocol-level pre-authentication foothold into organizations [record_id:68] [record_id:2088] [record_id:2146]. GPS-tracker research reports 45 vulnerabilities and compromise across nominally different brands sharing infrastructure [record_id:2646] [record_id:2903]. The Meari cloud audit goes further by describing twelve independent architectural evidence chains affecting 1.1 million cameras and more than 300 white-label brands [record_id:2949]. These are high-impact claims with CVE references, but independent validation and exact affected-version data remain necessary.

The tractor ECU work is notable for connecting safety recalls, firmware differential analysis, wireless accessibility, and undisclosed security impact [record_id:2609] [record_id:2943]. It also highlights a policy gap: users may understand a recall as reliability maintenance without learning that it closes an RCE path into a brake controller.

For defensive research, ICSForge is one of the most concrete tools because it specifies protocol coverage, ATT&CK mapping, safe sender-receiver architecture, and measurable validation questions [record_id:2995]. IRONMAP is notable for scale and protocol depth [record_id:3062]. OOBscan-backed BMC research is representative of how old embedded management protocols can undermine modern segmentation [record_id:2670] [record_id:2918].

The autonomous-defense records provide quantitative but simulation-bound evidence. The maritime MARL study reports performance under reduced alert-success probabilities [record_id:163], while NICSSIM reports response success, model latency, and compliance checks under explicit safety guardrails [record_id:3024]. These are useful baselines, but neither proves safe autonomous response in live plants.

Finally, the election-system records are important cyber-physical governance evidence. They cover manipulation without malware, weak physical controls, CARVER analysis, unreadable ballot encodings, insider access, legal research exemptions, and the legacy of top-to-bottom reviews [record_id:1855] [record_id:1856] [record_id:1858] [record_id:1859] [record_id:1865] [record_id:1867] [record_id:1868] [record_id:1869]. Their shared message is that recovery, auditability, system design, legal authority, and chain of custody matter as much as exploit sophistication.

## Gaps, Limits, And Open Questions

### Most evidence is abstract-level and self-reported

The records often provide only conference descriptions. Claims about device counts, exploit reliability, CVSS scores, global prevalence, and physical consequences generally lack code, packet captures, affected-version matrices, or independent reproduction in the supplied text. Future-dated 2026 records should be treated as planned presentations rather than completed, peer-reviewed evidence unless downstream researchers locate the promised artifacts.

Duplicate conference versions strengthen evidence that a research project was presented in multiple venues, but not that it was independently verified. Examples include ReVault [record_id:48] [record_id:1942], NVIDIA’s RISC-V security architecture [record_id:67] [record_id:1944], Axis camera research [record_id:68] [record_id:2088] [record_id:2146], OPC UA [record_id:7] [record_id:2140], EV modem research [record_id:1931] [record_id:2145], AirMax [record_id:2624] [record_id:2901], ZTP [record_id:2592] [record_id:2926], GPS trackers [record_id:2646] [record_id:2903], and PackBot [record_id:2596] [record_id:2886].

### Prevalence and exploitability are often conflated

Internet exposure does not necessarily mean a device is exploitable, safety-critical, or reachable in a useful operational context. Conversely, systems hidden behind gateways may still be discoverable or reachable through forwarding and remote-access paths. Downstream work should distinguish:
- number of responding hosts;
- number accurately identified as OT;
- number with exploitable versions;
- number connected to consequential processes;
- and number for which compromise can produce a verified physical effect.

IRONMAP, BMC, data-center, tunneling, and tracker datasets offer starting points, but their sampling, classification, duplicate handling, authorization, and false-positive controls are not described fully in the abstracts [record_id:9] [record_id:2670] [record_id:3010] [record_id:3062].

### Safety validation remains underdeveloped

Destructive testing and simulated impact are valuable, but there is no common framework across sectors for translating vulnerability primitives into bounded safety claims. A command-injection bug in a controller does not automatically imply catastrophic process impact; plant safeguards, mechanical interlocks, operator procedures, and independent protection systems may prevent or limit harm. The power-grid record explicitly warns that physical protections and system dynamics can dominate outcomes [record_id:2072] [record_id:3009].

Open questions include how to safely test worst-case conditions, how to incorporate process engineers into vulnerability assessment, and how to report physical impact without giving false certainty. Medical-device work particularly argues for behavior and essential-performance analysis beyond CVEs [record_id:3033].

### Remediation economics and operational constraints need more evidence

The records strongly advocate segmentation, patching, secure boot, authenticated updates, asset inventory, and lifecycle policy, but provide less evidence about cost, outage windows, vendor support, and measurable adoption. OT segmentation often erodes because production needs, maintenance pressure, and vendor access override design intent [record_id:3002]. End-of-life policy proposals remain largely conceptual [record_id:2764]. More comparative studies are needed on compensating controls for devices that cannot be patched or replaced.

### AI results need reproducibility, guardrails, and safety metrics

AI-assisted research has promising case studies but no shared benchmarks for embedded firmware, protocol reasoning, or hardware interaction. Questions include:
- Which tasks are reliable enough for automation?
- How should agents be prevented from modifying live systems?
- How should hallucinated register maps, protocols, and patches be detected?
- Can local models provide useful OT assistance under latency and memory constraints?
- How should AI-generated attack sequences be governed in labs and released publicly?

The contrast between failed embedded “vibe coding” [record_id:2206] and bounded ranking or waypoint workflows [record_id:2619] [record_id:2930] suggests that tool design matters more than model size. Physical-AI governance also remains immature because existing frameworks do not adequately cover adaptive systems capable of kinetic action [record_id:2348].

### Sector coverage is uneven

Maritime, automotive, consumer IoT, and space receive extensive attention. Traditional manufacturing processes, chemical plants, rail signaling, mining, pharmaceuticals, and upstream oil and gas receive less detailed technical coverage. Water-sector records are rich in governance and resilience but relatively thin on specific process-level vulnerability demonstrations [record_id:2427] [record_id:2436] [record_id:2845]. Food and cold-chain records identify concentration and refrigeration risk, but only the refrigeration-controller study provides substantial technical detail [record_id:2451] [record_id:2847] [record_id:3071].
