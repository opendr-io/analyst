# Topic: Network security and NDR

## Meta-Summary

The records portray network security as a contest over visibility, trust boundaries, and control of increasingly heterogeneous communications infrastructure. Conventional perimeter assumptions—trusted peers, authenticated tunnels, segmented networks, secure management planes, and benign cloud or collaboration services—repeatedly fail when exposed to adversarial traffic. Tunneling protocols can become spoofing and amplification infrastructure; routers, firewalls, VPN clients, provisioning systems, and out-of-band controllers can become initial-access paths; and legitimate conferencing, email, advertising, cloud storage, and radio networks can conceal command and control.

On the defensive side, the collection shows network detection and response moving beyond isolated signatures toward behavioral baselines, flow and protocol metadata, graphs, infrastructure fingerprints, deception, and automated analysis. Zeek logs, NetFlow, PCAPs, DNS, firewall logs, proxy records, TLS/HTTP fingerprints, cellular control-plane captures, and industrial-protocol telemetry are recurring evidence sources. Machine learning is common, but the strongest methodological records emphasize representative data, explicit object definition, causal experimental design, explainability, operational validation, and controlling false positives rather than treating a model as a substitute for sound telemetry.

The topic also extends far beyond enterprise Ethernet. Cellular, Wi-Fi, LoRa, fiber access networks, satellite links, maritime systems, aviation communications, industrial Ethernet, automotive networks, and radio alerting systems all appear. Across these settings, the same systemic weaknesses recur: unauthenticated control traffic, predictable protocol behavior, weak key lifecycle management, forgotten management interfaces, poorly enforced segmentation, and systems designed for availability or interoperability rather than hostile environments.

## Research Landscape

The collection is dominated by conference abstracts and talk descriptions from Black Hat, DEF CON, BSides Las Vegas, CAMLIS, and related villages. The CAMLIS material, concentrated between 2017 and 2025, is primarily methodological: feature engineering, intrusion-data collection, behavioral clustering, campaign extraction, alert prioritization, graph summarization, and reinforcement-learning approaches to network defense. ## Major Themes And Trends

### Network trust assumptions are becoming primary attack surfaces

A central theme is that network infrastructure often authenticates channels without validating the claims or behavior carried through them. Stateless tunnel protocols such as GRE, IPIP, 6in4, 4in6, and VXLAN can accept attacker-supplied traffic and turn exposed hosts into proxies, amplification nodes, or entry points. One study reports roughly 4.3 million susceptible hosts and introduces packet-looping and temporal-burst amplification techniques [record_id:9]. Related work describes tunnel injection for internal access, spoofed internal traffic, VXLAN hijacking, and subsequent manipulation of BGP or OSPF [record_id:1] [record_id:1907] [record_id:2021]. Later BGP research explicitly builds on tunnel injection to cross adjacency boundaries and reach vulnerable routing implementations [record_id:2921].

The same problem appears in newer control planes. Wi-Fi Automated Frequency Coordination trusts access points to report accurate location, time, DNS, and network context, permitting location spoofing, denial of service, response injection, and infrastructure exhaustion even where the nominal cryptographic channel remains intact [record_id:2676]. Zero-touch provisioning assumes a trustworthy relationship between controllers and managed devices; hard-coded keys, information disclosure, and RCE can instead make provisioning a mechanism for fleet-scale infiltration [record_id:2592] [record_id:2926]. GPON deployments blur the former boundary between the optical terminal and customer router, potentially allowing a compromised line terminal to push malicious firmware into subscriber premises [record_id:2663] [record_id:2914].

Industrial deterministic networking offers another form of misplaced trust. CC-Link IE TSN reportedly validates frames primarily through predictable timing and synchronization values rather than sender authenticity, allowing correctly timed injected frames to manipulate cyclic I/O while preserving expected timing behavior [record_id:2593]. TCP timestamps can likewise become unintended execution-time oracles, reducing network jitter enough to support remote Lucky13 and user-enumeration attacks [record_id:2626]. The broader 2026 “Breaking Trust Boundaries” record frames these findings as a systemic class of attacks against decades-old infrastructure assumptions rather than isolated implementation bugs [record_id:2640].

### Perimeter security devices are valuable targets, not neutral defenses

Firewalls, routers, VPNs, ZTNA clients, load balancers, and management controllers repeatedly appear as privileged footholds. A five-year campaign attributed to a China-based adversary reportedly used custom exploits and bespoke malware against multiple major firewall vendors, suggesting a sustained strategic focus on devices that sit outside normal endpoint visibility [record_id:56] [record_id:1998]. DrayTek router RCEs, legacy modem flaws, AirMax kernel-level wireless RCE, and TP-Link provisioning weaknesses reinforce the point [record_id:1906] [record_id:2120] [record_id:2624] [record_id:2901] [record_id:2592].

“Next-generation” access controls do not automatically remove legacy risk. GlobalProtect research describes VPN bypass and local privilege escalation rooted in client design choices [record_id:54]. ZTNA/SASE research reports authentication and posture bypasses, insecure client IPC, traffic-steering circumvention, and malicious-server attacks across several vendors [record_id:2062] [record_id:2136]. F5 devices can expose configuration secrets once the unit master key is extracted [record_id:2522]. Bare-metal clouds inherit provisioning, firmware, and network attacks capable of crossing tenant boundaries [record_id:1955].

BMCs are especially important because they remain reachable while hosts are powered off and have bidirectional hardware trust with the main system. Large scans found widespread RAKP hash disclosure, default or crackable passwords, and pre-authentication information leaks; compromise can persist through operating-system reinstalls and bridge management and production networks through internal buses [record_id:2670] [record_id:2918]. This supports the broader segmentation warning that out-of-band controllers, serial servers, dual-homed hosts, and protocol gateways can invalidate a firewall-centric network map [record_id:2828].

### Trusted services increasingly carry covert command and control

Several records show attackers hiding channels inside infrastructure that defenders are reluctant to block or inspect. TURNt routes short-lived, high-bandwidth C2 through conferencing-provider media servers that organizations often whitelist and exempt from TLS inspection [record_id:33] [record_id:1913]. Google Calendar and other Google services can carry RAT commands or SOCKS tunnels [record_id:2097]. SMTP and IMAP through Outlook can bypass environments that isolate or block workstation HTTP/HTTPS [record_id:2536]. C4 generalizes external C2 modules across trusted web services and implant languages [record_id:2117], while distributed storage such as IPFS, Codex, and Cloudflare R2 can host resilient commands, payloads, and phishing material [record_id:1896].

Other channels use less conventional networks. Meshtastic can provide low-bandwidth, decentralized shell access [record_id:1895]; AIS can carry covert fragments and C2 over a globally funded maritime network with little authentication or validation [record_id:2958]; and PhantomShell creates a bidirectional channel by sniffing legitimate TCP services and crafting responses that resemble the service’s own packets [record_id:3099]. Underground VPN infrastructure can itself be supply-chain backdoored to install rogue roots, hijack DNS, and intercept operator traffic [record_id:2879].

The defensive implication is that protocol or destination allowlisting is inadequate. Detection must examine service-to-workload attribution, timing, behavioral consistency, unexpected protocols, and deviations from the normal use of trusted platforms. This is reflected in work combining proxy logs, eBPF telemetry, and container metadata to identify the service responsible for an outbound connection [record_id:2761], and in application-specific behavior models trained from web traffic [record_id:23].

### DNS and infrastructure metadata remain exceptionally rich

DNS is simultaneously an exfiltration path, tracking mechanism, threat-intelligence source, and reconnaissance layer. Kernel-enforced DNS security proposes eBPF inspection and process termination to stop tunneling before packets leave an endpoint [record_id:35]. Earlier red-team-derived modeling treats DNS exfiltration as a supervised-learning problem [record_id:237], while security-specific encoders for domains, URLs, IPs, and related fields reportedly improve DNS classification from approximately 65% to over 90% [record_id:224].

At the privacy level, analysis of 1.5 billion requests from 30,000 mobile devices found that simple frequency and correlation statistics could fingerprint devices from partial DNS traces, with notable behavioral differences between iOS and Android [record_id:81]. Domain intelligence can also identify malicious infrastructure before domains become widely published IOCs [record_id:1877]. Certificate Transparency provides a complementary public view into domains and unintentionally exposed internal naming [record_id:2758].

Broader infrastructure research uses passive DNS, TLS certificates, host keys, HTTP assets, registry data, and longitudinal scans to cluster malicious systems. LightSpy infrastructure was expanded from one known server to 72 through certificate pivots [record_id:2659]. Cl0p infrastructure analysis identifies repeated hosting and ASN patterns across multiple campaigns [record_id:3050]. JARM, JA3/JA4, SSH host keys, and MurmurHash are presented as durable campaign fingerprints [record_id:3118]. A cross-domain IPv4 study combines RIR records, reverse DNS, services, sanctions data, and dated snapshots to expose inconsistent or deceptive registration and naming [record_id:3112].

### NDR is shifting from event detection to behavioral and relational analysis

The collection repeatedly argues that individual alerts are insufficient. Pairwise log comparison can group heterogeneous events into attack campaigns [record_id:193], while HeAT reconstructs stages leading to a critical IoC using analyst-defined relevance and network-agnostic features [record_id:196]. CLEAR-ROAD mines temporally recurring alert patterns without labeled training data [record_id:208]. Dynamic graphs and summarization can reduce incident-report size while preserving core entities and relationships [record_id:197]. Newer graph workshops make similar arguments using open-source graph tooling and AI coding assistants [record_id:2797].

Behavioral modeling occurs at several scales. Connected components over host, destination, URI, source, and user-agent features help define coherent webshell episodes before classification [record_id:174]. IP-level NetFlow distributions and quantiles are used to detect botnet C2 [record_id:179]. Asset subpopulation clustering provides prefilters and hunt enrichment in noisy enterprise networks [record_id:261]. BEAM models 65 application-level signals from over 40 billion transactions to detect supply-chain compromise through deviations in network behavior [record_id:23]. Finch uses JA3, JA4, JA4H, and HTTP/2 fingerprints to block or redirect bots and feed dynamic deception [record_id:1948].

The newer records add agentic automation but also retain human and operational constraints. Orbie applies an agent to internet-scale honeypot sessions while emphasizing that embedded domain expertise matters more than the chosen model [record_id:2365]. An AI hunting workshop translates manual routines such as DNS-tunnel and lateral-scan investigations into validated “SKILLs,” while explicitly addressing hallucinations, token cost, and false positives [record_id:2777]. Living Off the Land Engagements combine Caldera, Engage, OpenCanary, Blue Agave, and generated decoys to create environment-specific tripwires [record_id:2657].

### Data quality, causality, and generalization are recurring concerns

The strongest analytical records caution that security datasets are noisy, biased, and environment-dependent. Adaptive honeypot experiments use controlled intervention rather than observation alone, reporting at least 33% lower deployment time and improved attack collection efficiency [record_id:159]. The webshell case study emphasizes realistic threat emulation, overlaying malicious PCAPs on real enterprise traffic, careful unit-of-analysis construction, iterative cuts, and meta-validation [record_id:174]. Red-team exercises can provide labels, but the record explicitly raises selection bias and class imbalance [record_id:237].

Generalization remains difficult. TERLA attempts to transfer reinforcement-learning defense agents across network topologies without retraining [record_id:116]. HeAT similarly claims portability across networks [record_id:196]. A standardized flow-feature and dynamic-ensemble approach reports 97.92% detection across traffic classes [record_id:2390], but the broader collection shows why isolated headline accuracy is insufficient. Homoglyph-domain detection, for example, reports only 11–15% exact accuracy but may still be operationally useful when applied to a constrained monitored set [record_id:177]. Internet-service lifespan modeling is presented largely through failed approaches and adjustments rather than a simple success narrative [record_id:2505].

Autonomous defense research uses hierarchical reinforcement-learning agents and feature-ablation analysis [record_id:185], while exfiltration-path modeling adds protocol and payload properties to the Markov decision process [record_id:162]. Predictive defense prototypes model campaign progression and public precursor signals [record_id:232]. Collectively, these works support cautious, context-aware automation rather than unqualified replacement of analysts.

### Availability attacks are evolving across layers and domains

DDoS research spans low-and-slow application attacks, protocol amplification, server-state exhaustion, satellite networks, Windows RPC, and industrial networks. A layered testbed combines OS state, network traffic, application calls, user impact, supervised learning, time series, and signal processing to distinguish harmful low-volume attacks from benign load [record_id:258]. Tunneling hosts can create packet loops or temporally concentrated bursts [record_id:9]. Public domain controllers can reportedly be coerced through RPC and LDAP behaviors into denial-of-service roles [record_id:1940].

HTTP remains a major target. HTTP/2 server push and signed exchanges can weaken origin boundaries [record_id:21]; dead HTTP/2 streams can continue consuming backend work after the protocol layer considers them closed [record_id:2881]; HTTP/3 QPACK blocking and priority behavior can deterministically orchestrate backend race conditions [record_id:2651]; and CRLF-based desynchronization can produce request smuggling and response poisoning even without a traditional parser disagreement [record_id:2932]. Satellite constellations add dynamic topology, constrained bandwidth, and predictable movement, enabling targeted disruption with comparatively small botnets [record_id:2013].

### Wireless, cellular, and RF traffic are both attack surface and telemetry

The wireless records range from practical Wi-Fi training to protocol research. Workshops cover monitor mode, PCAP capture, rogue access points, captive portals, enterprise credential harvesting, WIDS validation, and tools such as Aircrack-ng, EAPHammer, hostapd-mana, Wifiphisher, and Nzyme [record_id:2578] [record_id:2700]. Passive Wi-Fi metadata can correlate randomized client identities using Information Elements, timing, sequence behavior, size, and channel context [record_id:3048]. Warflying and wardriving records explore large-area access-point observation [record_id:1887] [record_id:1893], while in-flight evil twins require correlation across Wi-Fi, ADS-B, satellite, portal, and airline infrastructure telemetry [record_id:1892].

Cellular security records include 5G user-plane trust failures [record_id:51], Open RAN interface exposure and 26 reported memory-corruption flaws [record_id:57], and Rayhunter’s use of Qualcomm diagnostic traffic to detect potential cell-site simulators [record_id:1889] [record_id:1922]. IoT cellular modules can be physically modified to intercept MCU-to-modem traffic and abuse trusted backend connectivity [record_id:44] [record_id:3058]. Maritime non-terrestrial cellular networks may inherit terrestrial 4G/5G weaknesses [record_id:3029].

LoRa and radio systems show similar issues. Meshtastic can be decoded and transmitted using SDR, GNU Radio, Python, and protocol knowledge [record_id:1884]; DEF CON’s mesh itself became a hacking target [record_id:2944]. A proposed emergency broadcast design deliberately avoids mesh-chat flooding and receiver transmissions in favor of signed one-way LoRa broadcasts [record_id:2843]. Other records cover unauthenticated public-alert signals [record_id:1894], radio key replacement attacks [record_id:1952], unauthenticated aviation messages [record_id:2861], and low-cost drone Remote ID sensing with conservative evidence fusion [record_id:3106].

### OT, maritime, aviation, and other cyber-physical networks are converging with IT

The collection documents broad OT/IT convergence without equivalent security modernization. OPC UA’s own cryptography may not eliminate the need for defense in depth; protocol flaws can create practical authentication bypasses [record_id:7] [record_id:2140]. Protocol gateways can conceal downstream CIP or DNP3 devices from ordinary inventory [record_id:2104]. OT DMZs are bypassed through remote access, jump hosts, Citrix, RDP relays, weak segmentation, and inherited credentials [record_id:2112]. Practical workshops therefore combine inventory, backups, network controls, hardening, and detection rather than presenting a single product as the solution [record_id:2719].

Maritime records are especially numerous. NMEA 2000 fingerprinting establishes deterministic device baselines in a small testbed [record_id:2049], while a later LSTM ensemble models timing, payload, frequency, and device behavior and reports an F1 of 0.964 on a 24-hour vessel capture [record_id:3057]. Other talks examine VSAT vulnerabilities [record_id:2039], SDN/SDR maritime communications [record_id:2045], virtual ship cyber ranges [record_id:2108], crane assessments and unexpected cellular modems [record_id:2065], and joint NOC/SOC asset discovery and threat hunting [record_id:3031]. Undersea cables and maritime connectivity are also treated as strategic resilience and governance problems [record_id:2011] [record_id:2966].

Aviation records expose unauthenticated AM radio and text-command systems [record_id:2047] [record_id:2861], while AFDX research provides a primer on Ethernet-derived flight-control networking [record_id:3005]. Automotive Ethernet can be disrupted through rogue DHCP and Layer 2/3 flooding [record_id:3054]. EV charging, smart buses, surveillance cameras, industrial switches, modems, and payment terminals all illustrate how network access can cross into safety, privacy, or physical control [record_id:1931] [record_id:2121] [record_id:2088] [record_id:2593] [record_id:1906] [record_id:2904].

## Methods, Tools, And Approaches Discussed

Network telemetry collection is the foundation of most defensive work. Zeek-style HTTP metadata, connection logs, and ATT&CK-enriched relationships support webshell detection, campaign reconstruction, and incident visualization [record_id:174] [record_id:193] [record_id:197]. NetFlow supports large-scale botnet classification through conventional and distributional features [record_id:179]. PCAP capture appears across Wi-Fi, cellular, radio, OT, and threat-emulation workflows [record_id:1922] [record_id:2578] [record_id:2995] [record_id:3106]. Firewall telemetry is operationalized through FLASI, which uses Vector.dev, Grafana, and VictoriaLogs or Elasticsearch to avoid SIEM ingestion economics forcing excessive filtering [record_id:2727].

Feature engineering approaches range from security-aware encoding of IPs, URLs, usernames, domains, and geography [record_id:224] to flow-level standardized statistics and class-specific model ensembles [record_id:2390]. Other records use quantile distributions [record_id:179], connected components [record_id:174], temporal pattern mining [record_id:208], dynamic graphs [record_id:197] [record_id:2797], character-image LSTMs [record_id:177], application behavior baselines [record_id:23], and passive packet-size or timing fingerprints [record_id:2638] [record_id:3048].

Protocol research commonly combines reverse engineering, emulation, fuzzing, and controlled hardware tests. O-RAN testing combines static analysis, dynamic tracing, dependency discovery, and constraint-driven inputs [record_id:57]. RPKI research replaces single-input fuzzing with coherent cryptographic object batches and instrumented execution feedback [record_id:2671]. AirMax, EV HomePlug, TETRA, Meshtastic, CPDLC, and proprietary surveillance protocols are reconstructed from firmware, RF captures, or protocol traces [record_id:60] [record_id:1884] [record_id:1931] [record_id:2088] [record_id:2624] [record_id:2861].

Internet-wide measurement uses ZMap, zdns, registry datasets, protocol-aware scanners, certificate pivots, passive DNS, and longitudinal enrichment [record_id:9] [record_id:2659] [record_id:2670] [record_id:3062] [record_id:3112]. The records repeatedly distinguish deep protocol enumeration from simple port scanning. IRONMAP and PLCDISCO, for instance, enumerate industrial identities, registers, and tags rather than merely counting open ports [record_id:3062]. OOBscan is intended to audit IPMI/BMC exposure [record_id:2670] [record_id:2918], while SSHamble assesses SSH implementation weaknesses [record_id:2100].

Defensive architecture includes kernel-level enforcement, attribution, deception, and validation. eBPF is used both to inspect DNS and terminate associated implants [record_id:35] and to connect egress traffic to containers and services [record_id:2761]. Azazel’s “Cyber Scapegoat” aims to delay attackers by actively engaging them [record_id:2397]. Finch performs fingerprint-based routing and deception [record_id:1948]. ICSForge generates safe, realistic traffic and PCAPs across ten industrial protocols to validate IDS, firewall, ACL, and ATT&CK coverage without attacking operational devices [record_id:2995]. MOSAICS applies monitoring, asset discovery, and behavioral detection to naval systems [record_id:2075].

The collection also includes operational training and range construction. Vulnerable network topologies can be generated through “Intent as Infrastructure” [record_id:2783]; realistic ship simulators need traffic generation, emulated subsystems, test access points, and analytics [record_id:2108]; and hands-on wireless and OT workshops give participants repeatable labs rather than only conceptual guidance [record_id:2578] [record_id:2700] [record_id:2719].

## Notable Talks, Records, And Evidence

The tunneling-host study is one of the collection’s strongest network-level records because it combines internet scanning, a quantified affected population, two new amplification constructions, and a device-crash finding with operational impact [record_id:9]. The related GRE/VXLAN work is notable for connecting tunnel injection to initial access, anti-forensics, internal spoofing, and BGP/OSPF manipulation [record_id:1] [record_id:1907]. The later BGP-adjacency talk shows the research line expanding from tunnel abuse into session-level routing trust and implementation disagreement [record_id:2921].

The webshell case study is a representative NDR methodology record. It does not merely propose a classifier; it explains how malicious episodes were manufactured, converted to PCAP, embedded in real traffic, extracted through Zeek, grouped into coherent analytical objects, and iteratively validated [record_id:174]. The adaptive honeypot study is similarly important because it directly addresses causal inference and resource efficiency in intrusion-data collection [record_id:159].

BEAM is notable for scale and for detecting application compromise without endpoint instrumentation. Its reported training base of more than 40 billion web transactions, 65 behavioral signals, and application-specific baselines makes it a strong example of network telemetry being used to monitor trusted software behavior [record_id:23]. However, its 95–99% reported accuracy remains an abstract claim without confusion matrices, attack prevalence, or independent replication in the record.

The BMC work is important because it revisits a mature protocol rather than assuming older findings were resolved. The reported exposure numbers, credential weaknesses, pre-authentication leaks, and host-to-management-plane pivoting provide a concrete challenge to segmentation assumptions [record_id:2670] [record_id:2918]. Similarly, the firewall campaign records show why appliances that sit at the perimeter require independent telemetry and coordinated vendor response [record_id:56] [record_id:1998].

The RPKI fuzzing research is technically significant for both routing security and vulnerability-discovery methodology. It reports 21 new vulnerabilities across major validators and introduces batch-oriented, syntax-aware fuzzing for interdependent signed objects [record_id:2671]. Its importance lies less in another list of CVEs than in showing that cryptographic validity requirements can defeat ordinary sequential fuzzing.

The DNS-device tracking study is notable for showing that encrypted or integrity-protected DNS mechanisms do not necessarily remove traffic-analysis leakage. Its dataset size and finding that simple statistics can outperform unnecessary complexity make it a useful counterpoint to the collection’s AI-heavy records [record_id:81]. The Thread/Matter study makes a similar point: packet-size determinism can reveal devices and building topology despite encrypted payloads [record_id:2638].

The maritime NMEA studies form a useful progression from deterministic fingerprints in a five-node laboratory [record_id:2049] to a multi-modal unsupervised ensemble evaluated against 9.16 million messages and six simulated attacks [record_id:3057]. ICSForge is a complementary practical contribution because it focuses on safely proving whether sensors and controls observe realistic industrial behaviors [record_id:2995].

Finally, the annual Black Hat NOC reports provide recurring operational context about building, stabilizing, and securing a hostile open conference network [record_id:98] [record_id:2689]. Their raw records lack the eventual statistics, but they are valuable longitudinal anchors for real-world network operations rather than laboratory-only security.

## Gaps, Limits, And Open Questions

Most records are proposals or abstracts, not peer-reviewed papers or complete incident reports. Reported accuracy, affected-host counts, CVEs, and operational impacts generally cannot be independently verified from the supplied text. Duplicate conference appearances should not be counted as separate confirmation. Future-event records from 2026 are especially prospective.

Encrypted traffic analysis is well represented, but the collection does not fully resolve how to balance detection against privacy. DNS, Thread, Wi-Fi, cellular, and certificate metadata can reveal users, devices, internal names, and physical behavior [record_id:81] [record_id:2638] [record_id:2758] [record_id:3048]. Defensive use of these signals needs governance, minimization, retention limits, and abuse controls that the technical abstracts rarely discuss.

ML evaluations are inconsistent. Several records provide headline accuracy or F1 values but little information about prevalence, drift, base rates, adversarial adaptation, deployment costs, or false-alert burden. Cross-network generalization is a recurring aspiration rather than a settled result. More replication across distinct organizations, protocols, and time periods is needed.

There is also limited evidence about sustained production operation. Kernel enforcement [record_id:35], AI-generated rules [record_id:2365], agentic hunting [record_id:2777], autonomous RL defense [record_id:116] [record_id:185], and dynamic deception [record_id:1948] could all introduce failure modes when models or policies act on high-volume live traffic. Open questions include rollback, auditability, adversarial poisoning, and safe human override.

For OT and cyber-physical systems, the gap between laboratory exploitability and process-level consequence remains important. Some records use real hardware or vessels, but many use simulations or small testbeds. Future research should measure safety impact, protocol timing effects, recovery behavior, and deployment constraints without putting physical processes at risk. Segmentation research also needs longitudinal evidence about configuration drift and organizational ownership, not only initial architecture [record_id:3002].

Finally, the collection strongly documents compromised “neutral” infrastructure—residential routers, relays, cloud hosts, ad platforms, botnets, and proxy networks—but offers no simple governance solution. Coordinated takedown can work [record_id:2550], yet resilient proxy services remain globally distributed [record_id:2794], and aggressive state intervention risks fragmentation and surveillance [record_id:2703] [record_id:2842]. Technical, legal, economic, and civil-liberties trade-offs remain open.
